# Professional Development Insights: Evidence Base

## Summary

The interactive studio is grounded in a cautious synthesis of three evidence sources. The sources converge on professional learning that is tied to a meaningful practice problem, provides opportunities to develop and rehearse techniques with social support and feedback, and continues beyond a single event through context-specific use and follow-up. These features should be understood as design guidance rather than universal guarantees. The quality of the content, local feasibility, leadership support, and implementation conditions remain decisive.

## Public-facing insights

| Insight | Practical design implication | Evidence base |
|---|---|---|
| **Successful PD combines connected learning experiences.** | Sequence an experience around one priority practice: examine authentic work, see an example, try it, discuss it with peers, receive feedback, and revisit it in real-world use. | Learning Policy Institute review [1] |
| **Balance knowledge, motivation, technique, and use.** | Audit the plan: How will participants learn the core idea, commit to a goal, rehearse a technique with feedback, and be prompted to use and monitor it in their work setting? | Education Endowment Foundation guidance [2] |
| **Coaching is an observation-and-feedback improvement model.** | Use focused observation and feedback to improve a clearly defined practice, and protect implementation quality as coaching expands rather than assuming more contact hours will improve outcomes. | Kraft, Blazar, & Hogan meta-analysis [3] |

## Interpretation boundaries

The Learning Policy Institute review identifies features shared by successful models; it does not establish that any isolated feature will work in every context. The Education Endowment Foundation frames several mechanism claims as likely or possible and describes its balanced-design finding as early and tentative. The coaching meta-analysis reports causal evidence for coaching programs overall, while its feature and dosage analyses are descriptive. Most of the evidence addresses teacher professional development and student outcomes; broader application should be evaluated in context.
